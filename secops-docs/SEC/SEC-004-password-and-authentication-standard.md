## 3. Purpose

This Standard sets the measurable requirements for Authentication of Users, devices and
processes to the Organization's Information Systems: password construction and management,
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), SSO, session management, account lockout, credential
storage, breached-password screening, and Authentication for APIs and machines. It
implements SEC-003 and is aligned with NIST SP 800-63B.

## 4. Scope

| Dimension | In scope |
|---|---|
| People | All Workforce Members and Third-Party personnel with accounts on Organization Information Systems; developers and System Owners who build or configure Authentication |
| Systems | Every Information System, Cloud Service, SaaS application, Endpoint, Mobile Device, network device, database and API that authenticates Users or processes, and every Organization-developed application that stores or verifies credentials, including customer-facing applications |
| Data | All authentication factors and Secrets (classified Restricted under SEC-002), including passwords, PINs, OTP seeds, private keys, tokens and API keys |
| Locations | All locations, including remote access |
| Third parties | Third Parties that authenticate to Organization systems, and Third Parties that provide identity services to the Organization |

## 5. Requirements

### 5.1 General

- **SEC-004.5.1.1** Every User SHALL authenticate with a unique identifier issued under SEC-003 before any access to non-Public information or functions is permitted.
- **SEC-004.5.1.2** Authentication factors SHALL be treated as Secrets, classified Restricted, and SHALL NOT be shared, disclosed, written down in unencrypted form, or transmitted in cleartext.
- **SEC-004.5.1.3** All transmission of authentication factors SHALL be protected by Encryption meeting SEC-006.
- **SEC-004.5.1.4** Authentication failures SHALL return a generic error that does not reveal whether the identifier or the factor was incorrect.

### 5.2 Password construction

- **SEC-004.5.2.1** User account passwords SHALL meet the minimum lengths in SEC-004.7.1. Passwords without MFA are permitted only under an approved Exception (CMP-003).
- **SEC-004.5.2.2** Systems SHALL accept passwords of at least 64 characters and SHALL accept all printing ASCII characters, the space character, and Unicode characters. Passwords SHALL NOT be truncated.
- **SEC-004.5.2.3** Systems SHALL NOT impose composition rules (for example, mandatory mixtures of character types) except as required by `[PCI]` SEC-004.5.2.4, and SHALL NOT offer password hints or knowledge-based security questions.
- **SEC-004.5.2.4** `[PCI]` Passwords for User accounts with access to the CDE SHALL contain both numeric and alphabetic characters (PCI DSS 8.3.6).
- **SEC-004.5.2.5** Every new or changed password SHALL be checked against the blocklist in section 5.6 before it is accepted.
- **SEC-004.5.2.6** Service Account passwords, where a password must be used, SHALL meet SEC-004.7.1 and SHALL be randomly generated by the PAM vault or a secrets manager under SEC-005; they SHALL NOT be human-chosen.
- **SEC-004.5.2.7** Initial and reset passwords SHALL be unique per User, randomly generated, delivered through a channel separate from the identifier, valid for no longer than 24 hours, and changed on first use (`[PCI]` PCI DSS 8.3.5).
- **SEC-004.5.2.8** Systems SHALL offer paste functionality in password fields so that password managers can be used. The Organization SHALL provide an approved password manager to all Workforce Members.

### 5.3 Password change and rotation

- **SEC-004.5.3.1** Passwords SHALL NOT be subject to forced periodic rotation where MFA is enforced for the account.
- **SEC-004.5.3.2** A password SHALL be changed, and all sessions for the account terminated, on any suspected or confirmed compromise, on discovery in a breach corpus (section 5.6), or on instruction from the SOC Manager.
- **SEC-004.5.3.3** `[PCI]` Where a password or passphrase is the only authentication factor for User access to the CDE, it SHALL be changed at least every 90 days, or the account's security posture SHALL be analyzed dynamically and access determined accordingly (PCI DSS 8.3.9).
- **SEC-004.5.3.4** `[PCI]` A new password SHALL NOT be the same as any of the last four passwords for CDE accounts (PCI DSS 8.3.7).
- **SEC-004.5.3.5** Self-service password reset SHALL require MFA or identity verification of equivalent strength; help-desk resets SHALL verify identity using a method recorded in the help-desk procedure that does not rely on knowledge-based questions alone.

### 5.4 Multi-Factor Authentication

- **SEC-004.5.4.1** MFA SHALL be required for all remote access, all privileged access, all CDE access, and all SSO applications; phishing-resistant MFA SHALL be required for privileged access.
- **SEC-004.5.4.2** MFA SHALL additionally be required for: access to email and collaboration platforms; access to any system holding Restricted data; administrative consoles of Cloud Services and SaaS; access to the Identity Provider's self-service portal; and access to source code repositories and CI/CD platforms (SEC-010).
- **SEC-004.5.4.3** Phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn platform or roaming authenticators, or PKI-based smart cards) SHOULD be used by all Workforce Members and SHALL be the only MFA method available for Privileged Accounts.
- **SEC-004.5.4.4** SMS and voice one-time passcodes SHALL NOT be used for privileged access, CDE access or access to Restricted data. Where used for other access, they SHALL be registered only as a recovery or fallback method and use SHALL be reported under section 8.
- **SEC-004.5.4.5** Push-notification MFA SHALL use number matching or equivalent challenge-binding, and SHALL be rate-limited to prevent MFA fatigue attacks (SEC-004.7.8).
- **SEC-004.5.4.6** MFA factors SHALL be independent, so that access to one factor does not grant access to another. `[PCI]` MFA systems SHALL not be susceptible to replay attacks, SHALL not be bypassable by any User including administrators unless documented and approved by management for a limited time, SHALL use at least two different factor types, and success of all factors SHALL be required before access is granted (PCI DSS 8.5.1).
- **SEC-004.5.4.7** `[PCI]` MFA SHALL be implemented for all non-console access into the CDE for personnel with administrative access and for all access into the CDE (PCI DSS 8.4.1, 8.4.2), and for all remote network access originating outside the Organization's network that could access or impact the CDE (PCI DSS 8.4.3).
- **SEC-004.5.4.8** Enrolment of a new MFA authenticator SHALL require Authentication with an existing factor or identity verification under SEC-003.5.2.2. Each new enrolment SHALL generate a notification to the User and an event to the SIEM (SEC-012).
- **SEC-004.5.4.9** MFA bypass, "remember this device" periods and trusted-location exemptions SHALL NOT exceed the values in SEC-004.7.9 and SHALL NOT apply to privileged access or CDE access.

### 5.5 Single Sign-On

- **SEC-004.5.5.1** All applications that support SAML 2.0 or OpenID Connect SHALL be integrated with the Organization's central Identity Provider before entering production. Local application accounts SHALL be disabled for Workforce Members once SSO is enabled, except Break-glass Accounts governed by SEC-005.
- **SEC-004.5.5.2** SaaS applications that do not support SSO SHALL be approved through SEC-001.5.6.2 only with an Exception under CMP-003, and SHALL enforce MFA natively.
- **SEC-004.5.5.3** SSO assertions and tokens SHALL be signed with algorithms approved in SEC-006; SAML assertions SHALL be signed and SHOULD be encrypted; OpenID Connect implementations SHALL use the authorization code flow with PKCE and SHALL NOT use the implicit flow.
- **SEC-004.5.5.4** Identity Provider sessions SHALL have a maximum lifetime of SEC-004.7.6, after which re-authentication with MFA is required. Conditional access policies SHALL require a managed or compliant device (SEC-008) for access to Confidential and Restricted data.
- **SEC-004.5.5.5** SSO deprovisioning SHALL be propagated to integrated applications by SCIM or equivalent automated provisioning wherever supported, so that SEC-003 revocation times are met.
- **SEC-004.5.5.6** The Identity Provider SHALL be configured with risk-based sign-in detection (impossible travel, anonymizing networks, known-malicious IPs, token anomalies), and high-risk sign-ins SHALL be blocked or require step-up phishing-resistant MFA.

### 5.6 Breached-password screening

- **SEC-004.5.6.1** Every new or changed password SHALL be compared against a blocklist containing: (a) passwords from known breach corpora; (b) dictionary words; (c) repetitive or sequential characters; and (d) context-specific words, including the Organization's name, product names and the User's identifier.
- **SEC-004.5.6.2** The breach corpus SHALL be updated at the frequency in SEC-004.7.10. Comparison against third-party breach services SHALL use a k-anonymity range query or an on-premises copy, so that the full password or its full hash is never sent to a Third Party.
- **SEC-004.5.6.3** Existing passwords SHALL be screened against the updated breach corpus at the frequency in SEC-004.7.10 where the credential store supports it, and Users whose passwords match SHALL be required to change their password at next sign-in.
- **SEC-004.5.6.4** The Identity Provider SHALL ingest leaked-credential detections where available, and matches SHALL be handled under SEC-004.5.3.2.

### 5.7 Account lockout and throttling

- **SEC-004.5.7.1** Accounts SHALL be locked after max 10 failed attempts, for min 30 minutes or until identity is verified.
- **SEC-004.5.7.2** Authentication endpoints exposed to the internet SHALL additionally apply rate limiting per source and per account, and bot detection or CAPTCHA after repeated failures.
- **SEC-004.5.7.3** Lockout events and password-spraying patterns (many accounts, few attempts each) SHALL be logged and alerted under SEC-012.

### 5.8 Session management

- **SEC-004.5.8.1** Interactive sessions SHALL be locked or terminated after 15 minutes of inactivity, requiring re-authentication to resume. `[HIPAA]` This implements automatic logoff under §164.312(a)(2)(iii).
- **SEC-004.5.8.2** Session identifiers SHALL be generated with at least 128 bits of entropy using an approved random bit generator (SEC-006), transmitted only over TLS, set with Secure, HttpOnly and SameSite attributes where cookies are used, and regenerated after Authentication and privilege change.
- **SEC-004.5.8.3** Sessions SHALL be invalidated server-side on logout, password change, account disablement, and MFA reset.
- **SEC-004.5.8.4** Re-authentication SHALL be required before high-risk actions in applications holding Restricted data (for example, changing payout or bank details, exporting bulk data, changing MFA settings).

### 5.9 Credential storage

- **SEC-004.5.9.1** Passwords SHALL be stored only as salted, one-way hashes produced by a memory-hard or adaptive password hashing function. Reversible encryption of stored passwords SHALL NOT be used.
- **SEC-004.5.9.2** The approved password hashing functions and minimum parameters are:

| Function | Minimum parameters | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Argon2id (RFC 9106) | Memory ≥ 19 MiB (19,456 KiB), iterations ≥ 2, parallelism 1; or memory ≥ 46 MiB, iterations ≥ 1, parallelism 1 | Preferred for all new systems |
| scrypt | N ≥ 2^17, r = 8, p = 1 | Permitted where Argon2id is unavailable |
| bcrypt | Cost factor ≥ 12; input limited to 72 bytes, so passwords longer than 72 bytes SHALL be pre-hashed with HMAC-SHA-256 keyed with a pepper | Permitted for existing systems |
| PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA-256 | ≥ 600,000 iterations | Only where a FIPS 140-3 validated implementation is required |

- **SEC-004.5.9.3** Each password hash SHALL use a unique salt of at least 128 bits from an approved random bit generator. A secret pepper SHOULD be applied, stored separately from the hash database in an HSM or KMS under SEC-006.
- **SEC-004.5.9.4** Hashing parameters SHALL be reviewed at the frequency in SEC-004.7.11 and increased so that a single hash computation takes at least 250 ms on production hardware, subject to capacity limits documented by the System Owner. Existing hashes SHALL be upgraded to new parameters at the User's next successful sign-in.
- **SEC-004.5.9.5** Hashes created with MD5, SHA-1, unsalted SHA-2, or any function not listed in SEC-004.5.9.2 SHALL be migrated by wrapping the legacy hash in an approved function immediately, and replaced with a native approved hash at the User's next sign-in.
- **SEC-004.5.9.6** OTP seeds and other authenticator secrets that must be retrievable SHALL be encrypted under SEC-006 with keys held in an HSM or KMS, separately from the data store.
- **SEC-004.5.9.7** `[PCI]` All authentication factors SHALL be rendered unreadable with strong cryptography during transmission and storage on all system components (PCI DSS 8.3.2).

### 5.10 Secrets in code and configuration

- **SEC-004.5.10.1** The prohibition on Secrets in source code, scripts, images, Infrastructure as Code, committed configuration, pipelines and logs is set in SEC-010.5.7.2. `[PCI]` Passwords for application and system accounts SHALL NOT be hard-coded in scripts, configuration or property files, or source code (PCI DSS 8.6.2).
- **SEC-004.5.10.2** Storage of Secrets in the approved secrets manager, Secret detection in repositories and pipelines, and revocation and rotation of exposed Secrets are governed by SEC-010. Privileged credentials are vaulted under SEC-005 and cryptographic keys are managed under SEC-006.

### 5.11 Authentication for APIs and machines

- **SEC-004.5.11.1** Machine-to-machine Authentication SHALL use, in order of preference: (1) workload identity federation or platform-managed identities issuing short-lived tokens; (2) mutual TLS with certificates issued by an Organization-controlled CA under SEC-006; (3) OAuth 2.0 client credentials with private-key JWT or mTLS client Authentication; (4) static API keys or Service Account passwords, only where (1)–(3) are not supported and recorded in the Service Account record (SEC-003.5.9.1).
- **SEC-004.5.11.2** OAuth 2.0 access tokens SHALL have a maximum lifetime of SEC-004.7.13. Refresh tokens SHALL be sender-constrained or rotated on use, and SHALL be revoked on detection of reuse.
- **SEC-004.5.11.3** JSON Web Tokens SHALL be signed with algorithms approved in SEC-006; the "none" algorithm SHALL be rejected; issuer, audience, expiry and not-before claims SHALL be validated by the relying service.
- **SEC-004.5.11.4** Static API keys and client secrets SHALL be at least 256 bits of entropy, generated by an approved random bit generator, scoped to the minimum permissions, bound to source IP ranges or workloads where the platform supports it, and rotated at least at the frequency in SEC-004.7.14 and on suspected compromise.
- **SEC-004.5.11.5** Every API exposed by the Organization that returns non-Public data SHALL authenticate every request. Anonymous access SHALL be limited to Public data.
- **SEC-004.5.11.6** Devices (Endpoints, servers, network devices) connecting to Organization networks holding Confidential or Restricted data SHALL authenticate using device certificates or equivalent device identity, where the platform supports it (SEC-007, SEC-008).
- **SEC-004.5.11.7** Service Account credentials SHALL NOT be used by individuals, and interactive login with them is governed by SEC-003.5.9.4.

### 5.12 Customer-facing applications

- **SEC-004.5.12.1** Organization-developed applications that authenticate customers SHALL meet sections 5.2, 5.6, 5.7, 5.8 and 5.9 of this Standard, SHALL offer MFA to all customers, and SHALL require MFA for customer administrative roles.
- **SEC-004.5.12.2** `[PCI]` Service providers SHALL meet PCI DSS 8.3.10 / 8.3.10.1 for customer User passwords where a password is the only factor.

## 7. Minimum standards

| ID | Requirement | Value |
|---|---|---|
| SEC-004.7.1 | Password length | Min 12 characters for user accounts with MFA; 15 without MFA (exception only); 32 random characters for service accounts |
| SEC-004.7.2 | Password rotation | No forced periodic rotation where MFA is enforced; change on suspected compromise; `[PCI]` 90 days where a password is the only factor |
| SEC-004.7.3 | MFA | Required for all remote access, all privileged access, all CDE access, and all SSO applications; phishing-resistant MFA for privileged access |
| SEC-004.7.4 | Account lockout | After max 10 failed attempts, for min 30 minutes or until identity is verified |
| SEC-004.7.5 | Idle session timeout | 15 minutes |
| SEC-004.7.6 | Maximum Identity Provider session lifetime before MFA re-authentication | 12 hours |
| SEC-004.7.7 | Maximum password length accepted | At least 64 characters |
| SEC-004.7.8 | Push MFA rate limit | Max 3 unanswered push prompts per 15 minutes, then the method is suspended for 1 hour and the SOC alerted |
| SEC-004.7.9 | Maximum "remember this device" period | 30 days on managed devices; 0 for privileged and CDE access |
| SEC-004.7.10 | Breach corpus update; re-screening of stored passwords | Monthly; monthly |
| SEC-004.7.11 | Password hashing parameter review | Every 12 months |
| SEC-004.7.12 | Revocation and rotation of an exposed Secret | Per SEC-010 |
| SEC-004.7.13 | Maximum OAuth 2.0 access token lifetime | 60 minutes |
| SEC-004.7.14 | Static API key and client secret rotation | Every 12 months, and on suspected compromise |
| SEC-004.7.15 | Initial / reset password validity | 24 hours; change on first use |
| SEC-004.7.16 | Minimum salt length; session identifier entropy; API key entropy | 128 bits; 128 bits; 256 bits |

## 9. Exceptions

Exceptions to this document are requested and approved under CMP-003.

The following SHALL NOT receive an Exception: MFA for privileged access and for `[PCI]` CDE
access (SEC-004.5.4.1, SEC-004.5.4.7); the prohibition on reversible password storage
(SEC-004.5.9.1); and the prohibition on hard-coded Secrets (SEC-004.5.10.1).

## 10. Enforcement

Non-compliance is handled under CMP-007.

The Head of Security Engineering MAY block sign-in for any account or application that does
not meet this Standard where continued access presents a Risk to Restricted data.
