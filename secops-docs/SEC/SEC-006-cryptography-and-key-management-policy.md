## 3. Purpose

This Policy sets the Organization's requirements for the use of cryptography to protect the
Confidentiality, Integrity and authenticity of information, and for the management of
cryptographic keys and certificates throughout their lifecycle. It defines the approved
algorithms and protocols, the controls over key generation, distribution, storage, rotation,
revocation and destruction, and the Organization's plan to migrate to post-quantum
cryptography.

## 5. Policy statements

### 5.1 Use of cryptography

- **SEC-006.5.1.1** Cryptography SHALL be used wherever SEC-002 requires Encryption at rest or in transit, wherever SEC-004 requires protection of authentication factors, and wherever integrity or authenticity of code, configuration or data must be assured (for example, code signing under SEC-010, signed Backups under SEC-018).
- **SEC-006.5.1.2** Only the algorithms, modes, key sizes and protocols listed as approved in section 5.2 SHALL be used. Prohibited algorithms and protocols SHALL NOT be used for any purpose for which they provide security.
- **SEC-006.5.1.3** Organization-developed software SHALL use cryptographic functions from vetted libraries and platform services listed in the cryptographic library register maintained by the Head of Security Engineering. Custom implementations of cryptographic primitives SHALL NOT be used.
- **SEC-006.5.1.4** Cryptographic modules protecting `[PCI]` CHD, `[HIPAA]` ePHI, Secrets, and other Restricted data SHALL be validated to FIPS 140-3. [ASSUMPTION: FIPS 140-2 certificates moved to the CMVP historical list in September 2026; modules relying solely on a historical FIPS 140-2 certificate require an Exception under CMP-003 with a replacement date.]
- **SEC-006.5.1.5** Random numbers used for keys, nonces, initialization vectors, salts, tokens and session identifiers SHALL be generated by an approved deterministic random bit generator (NIST SP 800-90A) seeded from an approved entropy source, or by the operating system's cryptographically secure generator built on such a generator.
- **SEC-006.5.1.6** The cryptographic library register, approved algorithm list and cipher-suite configurations SHALL be reviewed by the Head of Security Engineering at least every 12 months and on publication of a relevant cryptanalytic result, NIST deprecation or CA/Browser Forum change. `[PCI]` This review covers the inventory of cipher suites and protocols in use (PCI DSS 12.3.3).

### 5.2 Approved algorithms and protocols

- **SEC-006.5.2.1** The encryption minimums are: AES-256 at rest (AES-128-GCM minimum); TLS 1.2 minimum, TLS 1.3 preferred; RSA ≥ 3072 bits; ECC ≥ P-256; SHA-256 or stronger. Prohibited: SSL, TLS < 1.2, DES, 3DES, RC4, MD5, SHA-1 for signatures.
- **SEC-006.5.2.2** The approved algorithms and protocols are:

| Function | Approved | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Symmetric encryption at rest | AES-256-GCM, AES-256-XTS (storage devices), AES-256-CBC with HMAC-SHA-256 (encrypt-then-MAC) | AES-128-GCM is the minimum and permitted only where AES-256 is not supported by the platform |
| Authenticated encryption in transit / application | AES-GCM (128 or 256), AES-CCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305 | ChaCha20-Poly1305 SHALL NOT be used where FIPS 140-3 validation is required (SEC-006.5.1.4) |
| Asymmetric encryption / key transport | RSA-OAEP ≥ 3072 bits | RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 encryption SHALL NOT be used in new designs |
| Key establishment | ECDH / ECDHE on P-256, P-384, P-521; X25519; ML-KEM-768 or ML-KEM-1024 (FIPS 203), including hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-768 | X25519 permitted only outside FIPS-validated scope; finite-field DH ≥ 3072 bits only for legacy interoperability under Exception |
| Digital signatures | RSA-PSS or RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 signatures ≥ 3072 bits; ECDSA P-256 / P-384 / P-521; EdDSA Ed25519 / Ed448 (FIPS 186-5); ML-DSA-65 or ML-DSA-87 (FIPS 204); SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) | SHA-1 SHALL NOT be used in any signature |
| Hash functions | SHA-256, SHA-384, SHA-512, SHA-512/256, SHA3-256, SHA3-384, SHA3-512 | MD5 and SHA-1 permitted only for non-security purposes (for example, non-adversarial checksums, legacy HMAC-SHA-1 in existing protocols under Exception) |
| Message authentication | HMAC-SHA-256 or stronger; KMAC; AES-GMAC; AES-CMAC | Keys ≥ 256 bits for HMAC |
| Key derivation | HKDF (SP 800-56C), SP 800-108 KDFs | Password hashing is specified in SEC-004 |
| Key wrapping | AES-KW / AES-KWP (SP 800-38F), AES-GCM with KEK ≥ the strength of the wrapped key | |
| Random bit generation | SP 800-90A DRBG (CTR_DRBG, Hash_DRBG, HMAC_DRBG) | Dual_EC_DRBG prohibited |
| Transport protocols | TLS 1.3; TLS 1.2 with ECDHE and AEAD cipher suites only; SSH-2 with approved algorithms; IPsec IKEv2 with approved algorithms; SFTP / FTPS over approved TLS or SSH | TLS 1.2 renegotiation and compression disabled; RSA key exchange and CBC-mode suites disabled; SSH-1, IKEv1 aggressive mode, SNMPv1/v2c, Telnet, FTP, HTTP for non-Public data prohibited |
| Storage Encryption | Full-disk or volume Encryption with AES-256-XTS; database transparent Encryption; field- or application-level Encryption, tokenization for `[PCI]` PAN (SEC-019) | `[PCI]` Disk-level Encryption alone SHALL NOT be relied on to render PAN unreadable except on removable electronic Media (PCI DSS 3.5.1.2) |
| Email | TLS for server-to-server transport (SEC-014); S/MIME or approved secure messaging for Restricted content | |

- **SEC-006.5.2.3** Any algorithm or protocol not listed in SEC-006.5.2.2 SHALL be treated as not approved until the Head of Security Engineering adds it to the list.
- **SEC-006.5.2.4** Externally facing TLS endpoints SHALL be scanned for protocol and cipher-suite compliance at the frequency in SEC-006.7.10, and non-compliant configurations SHALL be remediated as High findings under SEC-011.
- **SEC-006.5.2.5** `[PCI]` Card-present POS POI terminals, and the termination points they connect to, SHALL use TLS 1.2 or higher. A terminal that supports only SSL or early TLS SHALL be replaced; until replaced, reliance on the PCI DSS Appendix A2 allowance SHALL require an Exception under CMP-003 supported by documented confirmation that the terminal is not susceptible to known exploits for those protocols (PCI DSS A2.1).
- **SEC-006.5.2.6** DKIM signing keys SHALL meet the RSA minimum in SEC-006.5.2.1. Where a sending platform supports only shorter RSA keys, RSA 2048-bit keys MAY be used only under an approved Exception under CMP-003; RSA keys shorter than 2048 bits SHALL NOT be used.

### 5.3 Key management roles and inventory

- **SEC-006.5.3.1** The Head of Security Engineering SHALL maintain a key and certificate inventory recording, for every key or key family: key identifier; purpose (data-encryption key, key-encryption key, signing, TLS, authentication); algorithm and length; owning System Owner; storage location (HSM, KMS, application); Cryptoperiod and activation, rotation and expiry dates; custodians; and associated systems and data Classification Level.
- **SEC-006.5.3.2** Key management functions SHALL be separated from data access functions: individuals who administer HSMs or KMS SHALL NOT, by that role, have access to the data the keys protect, and System Owners who use keys SHALL NOT be able to export or delete key material.
- **SEC-006.5.3.3** Key Custodians SHALL be designated in writing by the Head of Security Engineering, limited to the fewest individuals necessary, and SHALL acknowledge their responsibilities in writing before taking custody. `[PCI]` Key Custodians SHALL formally acknowledge (in writing or electronically) that they understand and accept their key-custodian responsibilities (PCI DSS 3.7.8).
- **SEC-006.5.3.4** `[PCI]` Service providers SHALL maintain a documented description of the cryptographic architecture, including algorithms, protocols and keys used to protect stored account data with key strength and expiry date, key usage, and an inventory of HSMs, KMS and other secure cryptographic devices (PCI DSS 3.6.1.1).

### 5.4 Key generation

- **SEC-006.5.4.1** Keys SHALL be generated inside an HSM or KMS validated to FIPS 140-3, or, for keys not protecting Restricted data, by an approved library using an approved random bit generator.
- **SEC-006.5.4.2** Root and intermediate Certificate Authority keys, `[PCI]` key-encryption keys protecting CHD, and master keys of the KMS hierarchy SHALL be generated in an HSM validated to FIPS 140-3 Level 3, in a documented key ceremony witnessed by at least two authorized individuals, with the ceremony record retained per CMP-004.
- **SEC-006.5.4.3** Key strength SHALL meet section 5.2. Key-encryption keys SHALL be at least as strong as the data-encryption keys they protect (`[PCI]` PCI DSS 3.6.1).
- **SEC-006.5.4.4** Each key SHALL be generated for a single purpose. The same key SHALL NOT be used for both encryption and signing, or across production and non-production environments.

### 5.5 Key distribution

- **SEC-006.5.5.1** Keys SHALL be distributed only in encrypted (wrapped) form under a key-encryption key, through an authenticated channel, or as key components or shares under split knowledge.
- **SEC-006.5.5.2** Keys SHALL be distributed only to the systems and custodians recorded in the key inventory as authorized to receive them, and receipt SHALL be confirmed and logged.
- **SEC-006.5.5.3** Cleartext keys SHALL NOT be transmitted by email, messaging, ticketing systems or any channel not approved under this section.

### 5.6 Key storage

- **SEC-006.5.6.1** Secret and private keys protecting Restricted data SHALL be stored only: (a) inside an HSM or KMS validated to FIPS 140-3 Level 2 or higher; (b) encrypted under a key-encryption key that is at least as strong and stored separately; or (c) as at least two full-length key components or shares under split knowledge. `[PCI]` This implements PCI DSS 3.6.1.2.
- **SEC-006.5.6.2** Keys SHALL be stored in the fewest possible locations (`[PCI]` PCI DSS 3.6.1.4), and key-encryption keys SHALL be stored separately from the data-encryption keys they protect.
- **SEC-006.5.6.3** Access to keys and key-management functions SHALL be restricted to the fewest custodians and services necessary, authorized under SEC-003 and SEC-005, and every use of a KMS or HSM key for administrative operations SHALL be logged under SEC-012.
- **SEC-006.5.6.4** Cloud KMS keys protecting Restricted data SHALL be customer-managed keys under Organization control, with key policies that prevent the Cloud Service Provider's operators from using them outside the service. [DECISION REQUIRED: Whether customer-held keys (external key management or bring-your-own-key) are required for any data set. Recommended default: customer-managed keys in the provider's FIPS 140-3 validated KMS; external key management only where a contract or regulator requires it]
- **SEC-006.5.6.5** Private keys of Workforce Members' certificates (for example, smart cards, FIDO2 credentials) SHALL be non-exportable from the authenticator.
- **SEC-006.5.6.6** Keys SHALL be backed up only in encrypted form or as split components, under the same or stronger protections as the primary, so that encrypted Backups remain recoverable throughout their retention period under CMP-004.

### 5.7 Cryptoperiods and rotation

- **SEC-006.5.7.1** Key Cryptoperiods SHALL be: data-encryption keys 12 months; key-encryption keys 24 months; immediate rotation on suspected compromise.
- **SEC-006.5.7.2** Rotation SHALL be automated in the KMS wherever supported. After rotation, the retired key version SHALL be used only to decrypt or verify existing data until that data is re-encrypted or reaches the end of retention.
- **SEC-006.5.7.3** Signing keys, TLS keys and other key types SHALL follow the Cryptoperiods in SEC-006.7.3, which are derived from NIST SP 800-57 Part 1.
- **SEC-006.5.7.4** `[PCI]` Keys SHALL be changed at the end of their defined Cryptoperiod (PCI DSS 3.7.4), and retired or replaced when the integrity of the key has been weakened, including on departure of a custodian with knowledge of a cleartext key component (PCI DSS 3.7.5).

### 5.8 Key revocation and compromise

- **SEC-006.5.8.1** Any suspected compromise of a key or certificate SHALL be reported to the SOC Manager immediately and handled as a Security Incident under RES-001.
- **SEC-006.5.8.2** On suspected compromise, the key SHALL be revoked or disabled for new operations, a replacement key generated and deployed, affected data re-encrypted or re-signed, and associated certificates revoked with the issuing CA, within the times in SEC-006.7.6.
- **SEC-006.5.8.3** The Head of Security Engineering SHALL maintain and test, at the frequency in SEC-006.7.11, a key-compromise response runbook covering the KMS master key, root CA key, code-signing keys and TLS keys of internet-facing services.

### 5.9 Key destruction

- **SEC-006.5.9.1** Keys that have reached the end of their Cryptoperiod and are no longer required to decrypt or verify retained data SHALL be destroyed using the HSM or KMS destruction function, or, for keys outside an HSM or KMS, by Media Sanitization under SEC-017.
- **SEC-006.5.9.2** Destruction of a key that is the sole means of decrypting retained data SHALL be approved by the Data Owner and checked against Legal Holds by the GC before execution. Intentional destruction to render data unrecoverable (cryptographic erasure) is governed by SEC-017.
- **SEC-006.5.9.3** Each key destruction SHALL be logged with key identifier, date, method, and the individuals performing and approving it.
- **SEC-006.5.9.4** Cloud KMS key deletion SHALL use the maximum pending-deletion waiting period offered by the provider, up to 30 days, to allow recovery from erroneous deletion.

### 5.10 Dual control and split knowledge

- **SEC-006.5.10.1** Manual cleartext key-management operations (generation, loading, export, backup, recovery, destruction) on Restricted keys SHALL be performed under dual control and split knowledge, so that no single individual can access or use the full cleartext key.
- **SEC-006.5.10.2** `[PCI]` Where manual cleartext cryptographic key-management operations are performed by personnel, they SHALL be managed using split knowledge and dual control, including at minimum key generation, distribution, loading, and destruction (PCI DSS 3.7.6). Access to cleartext key components SHALL be restricted to the fewest custodians necessary (PCI DSS 3.6.1.3).
- **SEC-006.5.10.3** `[PCI]` Key-management procedures SHALL cover: generation of strong keys (3.7.1); secure distribution (3.7.2); secure storage (3.7.3); changes at end of Cryptoperiod (3.7.4); retirement, replacement or destruction (3.7.5); split knowledge and dual control (3.7.6); prevention of unauthorized substitution (3.7.7); and custodian acknowledgement (3.7.8). `[PCI]` Service providers sharing keys with customers SHALL document guidance on secure transmission, storage and update of those keys (3.7.9).
- **SEC-006.5.10.4** `[PCI]` Controls SHALL prevent unauthorized substitution of keys, including HSM role separation, signed key-loading, and logging of every key import (PCI DSS 3.7.7).
- **SEC-006.5.10.5** Key components and shares SHALL be stored in separate tamper-evident containers in separate locked safes, each accessible only to its designated custodian, with access logged.

### 5.11 Certificate management

- **SEC-006.5.11.1** All TLS, code-signing, device, client-authentication and S/MIME certificates SHALL be recorded in the key and certificate inventory, discovered automatically at least weekly across internal networks and external domains, and reconciled monthly.
- **SEC-006.5.11.2** Publicly trusted TLS certificate lifetimes SHALL be no longer than the CA/Browser Forum maximum in force (200 days from 15 March 2026). Certificates issued by Organization private CAs SHALL have the maximum lifetimes in SEC-006.7.8.
- **SEC-006.5.11.3** Certificate issuance and renewal SHALL be automated (for example, using ACME or a certificate lifecycle management platform) for all endpoints that support it. Renewal SHALL complete no later than the lead time in SEC-006.7.9 before expiry.
- **SEC-006.5.11.4** Publicly trusted certificates SHALL be obtained only from Certificate Authorities on the approved CA list maintained by the Head of Security Engineering. DNS CAA records SHALL restrict issuance for Organization domains to approved CAs.
- **SEC-006.5.11.5** Wildcard certificates SHALL NOT be used for systems holding Restricted data or in `[PCI]` CDE scope, and SHALL otherwise be approved by the Head of Security Engineering with the list of hosts that hold the private key.
- **SEC-006.5.11.6** Certificates SHALL be revoked within the time in SEC-006.7.6 when the private key is suspected compromised, the subject is decommissioned, or the information in the certificate is no longer accurate.
- **SEC-006.5.11.7** Organization root CA keys SHALL be kept offline in an HSM, and issuing CAs SHALL be online only in a dedicated, Tier 0-administered environment (SEC-005).
- **SEC-006.5.11.8** `[PCI]` An inventory of the Organization's trusted keys and certificates used to protect PAN during transmission SHALL be maintained, and certificates used for PAN transmission over open, public networks SHALL be confirmed valid and not expired or revoked (PCI DSS 4.2.1, 4.2.1.1).

### 5.12 Crypto-agility

- **SEC-006.5.12.1** New systems and Organization-developed software SHALL be designed so that algorithms, key sizes and protocols are configurable without code changes, and SHALL record algorithm identifiers with protected data or signatures so that data can be migrated.
- **SEC-006.5.12.2** The CTO SHALL ensure that SBOMs produced under SEC-010 identify cryptographic libraries and their versions, so that vulnerable or deprecated cryptography can be located across the estate.
- **SEC-006.5.12.3** On deprecation of an approved algorithm, the Head of Security Engineering SHALL publish a migration deadline and track migration as a remediation item under SEC-011.

### 5.13 Post-Quantum Cryptography

- **SEC-006.5.13.1** The Head of Security Engineering SHALL maintain a cryptographic inventory, as an extension of the key and certificate inventory, identifying every use of quantum-vulnerable public-key cryptography (RSA, finite-field and elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman, ECDSA, EdDSA) by system, protocol, library, data Classification Level, data confidentiality lifetime and vendor dependency. The inventory SHALL be complete within the time in SEC-006.7.12 and updated at least every 12 months.
- **SEC-006.5.13.2** Each inventory entry SHALL be prioritized for migration using: (a) the period for which the protected data must remain confidential, compared against expected quantum-capable adversary timelines ("harvest now, decrypt later" exposure); (b) the Classification Level; (c) exposure to untrusted networks; and (d) the lifetime of the system or device.
- **SEC-006.5.13.3** The Head of Security Engineering SHALL maintain a post-quantum migration plan, approved by the CISO and reported to the ISSC at least every 12 months, that adopts NIST FIPS 203 (ML-KEM) for key establishment and NIST FIPS 204 (ML-DSA) and NIST FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA) for digital signatures, and aligns with the NIST transition timeline proposed in NIST IR 8547 (initial public draft, November 2024), under which quantum-vulnerable algorithms are deprecated after 2030 and disallowed after 2035. [ASSUMPTION: NIST IR 8547 is not yet final; the Head of Security Engineering SHALL update the plan within 90 days of its final publication if the timeline changes.]
- **SEC-006.5.13.4** Externally facing TLS endpoints and remote-access VPNs carrying Confidential or Restricted data SHALL enable hybrid post-quantum key establishment (for example, X25519 + ML-KEM-768) by the date in SEC-006.7.13 where the platform supports it.
- **SEC-006.5.13.5** Procurement of new Information Systems, HSMs, PKI and network security devices from the effective date of this Policy SHALL require a vendor roadmap for support of FIPS 203, 204 and 205, assessed under RSK-003.
- **SEC-006.5.13.6** Long-lived signatures (firmware, code signing, document signing with a validity beyond 2030) SHOULD use ML-DSA, SLH-DSA, or hybrid signatures once supported by the signing platform.

### 5.14 Third Parties and Cloud Services

- **SEC-006.5.14.1** Contracts with Third Parties that encrypt Organization Restricted data SHALL require compliance with section 5.2, FIPS 140-3 validated modules, and notification of key compromise within the period required under RSK-003.
- **SEC-006.5.14.2** Cloud Service Encryption configurations are specified in SEC-009 and SHALL meet this Policy.

## 9. Exceptions

Exceptions to this document are requested and approved under CMP-003.

The following SHALL NOT receive an Exception: use of SSL, TLS below 1.2, DES, RC4 or MD5 for
any security purpose on systems holding Restricted data; storage of cleartext Restricted keys
outside the options in SEC-006.5.6.1; and `[PCI]` split knowledge and dual control for manual
cleartext key operations (SEC-006.5.10.2).

## 10. Enforcement

Non-compliance is handled under CMP-007.

The Head of Security Engineering MAY require the disconnection of any system using a
prohibited algorithm or protocol for Restricted data until remediated.
